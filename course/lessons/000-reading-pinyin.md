# Lesson 0 · Reading Hakka pinyin when you already know Mandarin pinyin

The course writes Hakka in the Ministry of Education's romanisation (臺灣客家語拼音方案), the one used by the MOE
dictionary, the certification lists and school textbooks. It was designed to look like Mandarin pinyin, and most
letters do sound the same. The tones are where it differs, and they follow regular rules from Mandarin. This lesson is
the map; come back to it. Readings below are Sixian (四縣), the course's reference accent; §8 covers Hailu (海陸), which
is written alongside in every lesson.

## 1. Consonants: mostly pinyin

| Hakka | Sounds like | As in pinyin? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **b / p** | unaspirated / aspirated p | yes (b / p) | 八 badˋ · 爬 paˇ |
| **d / t** | unaspirated / aspirated t | yes | 打 daˋ · 天 tienˊ |
| **g / k** | unaspirated / aspirated k | yes | 家 gaˊ · 看 kon |
| **z / c / s** | ts / tsʰ / s | yes (z / c / s) | 豬 zuˊ · 坐 coˊ · 三 samˊ |
| **j / q / x** | only before i, as in pinyin | yes | 千 qienˊ · 心 ximˊ |
| **f** | f | yes | 花 faˊ · 火 foˋ |
| **v** | a real v (lips and teeth) | **no**: pinyin w | 文 vunˇ · 屋 vugˋ |
| **ng** | ng, also at the **start** of a syllable | **no** | 人 nginˇ · 牛 ngiuˇ · 你 ngiˇ |
| **h** | h | yes | 好 hoˋ · 去 hi |
| **m, n, l** | as in English | yes | 毋 mˇ · 來 loiˇ |

Sixian has **no zh, ch, sh, r**: Mandarin 豬 zhū, 茶 chá, 書 shū are Hakka 豬 zuˊ, 茶 caˇ, 書 suˊ. Hailu has them (§8).
The consonant to train first is **ng-** at the start of a word: 𠊎 ngaiˇ "I", 人 nginˇ, 日 ngidˋ.

## 2. Vowels and endings

| Hakka | Sound | Example |
|---|---|---|
| a, i, o, u | as in pinyin a, i, o, u | 花 faˊ · 去 hi · 火 foˋ · 書 suˊ |
| **e** | always [e] (like *é*), never pinyin e [ɤ] | 係 he · 个 ge |
| **ii** | the buzzing vowel of pinyin zi, ci, si, written ii and used after more consonants | 子 ziiˋ · 時 siiˇ · 字 sii |
| oi, ai, eu, ieu, ui | diphthongs, as spelled | 來 loiˇ · 狗 gieuˋ · 頭 teuˇ |
| **-m** | lips close at the end (Mandarin lost it) | 三 samˊ · 心 ximˊ · 甜 tiamˇ |
| **-n, -ng** | as in pinyin | 天 tienˊ · 聽 tangˊ |
| **-b -d -g** | **unreleased** p, t, k: the syllable is cut off short (as in Cantonese) | 汁 ziibˋ · 八 badˋ · 六 liugˋ |
| **m, n, ng** alone | a syllable with no vowel | 毋 mˇ "not" · 你 nˇ · 魚 ngˇ · 五 ngˋ |

**-b -d -g are not voiced and not released.** They are the old "entering tone" endings; the Hakka letters b d g
mark them, Cantonese and Taigi write p t k. Sixian also has finals no other Taiwanese Hakka has: **-iim, -iin, -iib,
-iid** (針 ziimˊ, 深 ciimˊ, 汁 ziibˋ, 直 ciid).

## 3. Tones: six of them, and the marks move one step from Mandarin

| Tone | Mark | Shape | Example | Mandarin words of this tone usually have… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 陰平 | **ˊ** (24) | low rising | 花 faˊ · 天 tienˊ · 三 samˊ | **tone 1** (67%) |
| 陽平 | **ˇ** (11) | **low level** | 人 nginˇ · 來 loiˇ · 頭 teuˇ | **tone 2** (75%) |
| 上聲 | **ˋ** (31) | falling from mid | 火 foˋ · 狗 gieuˋ · 講 gongˋ | **tone 3** (69%) |
| 去聲 | (none) (55) | **high level** | 去 hi · 看 kon · 大 tai | **tone 4** (72%) |
| 陰入 | **ˋ** + b/d/g (2) | low, cut short | 八 badˋ · 一 idˋ · 屋 vugˋ | (any tone: an old entering-tone character) |
| 陽入 | (none) + b/d/g (5) | high, cut short | 十 siib · 食 siid · 學 hog | (any tone) |

Percentages measured on 1,404 everyday characters (HSK readings against the MOE dictionary).

**The traps for a pinyin reader:**
- **ˇ is not a dip**: it is low and flat. 人 nginˇ stays low.
- **No mark means high and level** (like Mandarin tone 1), not "neutral".
- **ˊ rises** like pinyin tone 2 (lucky), but it goes on words that are tone 1 in Mandarin (花 huā → faˊ).
- **ˋ falls**, as in pinyin tone 4, but starts lower; it goes on Mandarin tone 3 words (火 huǒ → foˋ).
- About one Mandarin tone 3 word in six has ˊ instead, mostly words starting with m, n, l or no consonant:
  馬 maˊ, 冷 langˊ, 有 iuˊ, 買 maiˊ, 尾 miˊ.

So the rough rule is a **one-step shift**: Mandarin 1 2 3 4 → Hakka ˊ ˇ ˋ (none). Use it to guess, check in the word list.

## 4. Tone sandhi: one rule (and a half)

Sixian has the simplest sandhi of all Hakka (MOE workbook, lesson 25; knowledge article 107_015):

```
陰平 ˊ (24) → ˇ (11)   when the next syllable is 陰平 ˊ, 去聲 (none) or 陽入 (none + b/d/g)
                       before 陽平 ˇ, 上聲 ˋ or 陰入 ˋ it stays ˊ
```

| Written | Said | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 天光 tienˊ gongˊ | tienˇ gongˊ | ˊ before ˊ |
| 添飯 tiamˊ fan | tiamˇ fan | ˊ before 去聲 |
| 三月 samˊ ngied | samˇ ngied | ˊ before 陽入 |
| 阿婆 aˊ poˇ | aˊ poˇ | before ˇ: no change |
| 阿姆 aˊ meˊ | aˇ meˊ | ˊ before ˊ |

The course always **writes the citation tone** (as the dictionary does) and shows the spoken one where it matters.
Sandhi works **inside a phrase**, not across a pause: in 麻油炒豬肝 | 盡補 ("pig liver fried in sesame oil | is very
nourishing") 豬 zuˊ becomes zuˇ before 肝 gonˊ, but 肝 keeps ˊ before 盡 qin because a phrase ends there. The lesson
files mark such breaks with `|`; punctuation is always a break.

**The half rule, 仔 eˋ:** after a 上聲 ˋ or a 陰入 ˋ syllable, the 仔 suffix is read eˇ: 草仔 coˋ eˇ, 鐵仔 tiedˋ leˇ.

## 5. Linking: 仔 copies the sound before it

The noun suffix 仔 (eˋ) takes the end of the previous syllable as its first sound. You hear it; the word lists and
the course still write eˋ (豬仔 zuˊ eˋ is said zuˊ veˋ):

| Before | 仔 is said | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| -a, -o, -e, -ii | eˋ | 遮仔 zaˊ eˋ (umbrella) · 雞仔 gieˊ eˋ |
| -u | veˋ | 豬仔 zuˊ veˋ |
| -i | ieˋ | 姨仔 iˇ ieˋ |
| -m | meˋ | 柑仔 gamˊ meˋ |
| -n | neˋ | 粄仔 banˋ neˇ |
| -ng | ngeˋ | 羊仔 iongˇ ngeˋ |
| -b / -d / -g | beˋ / leˋ / geˋ | 盒仔 hab beˋ · 鐵仔 tiedˋ leˇ · 桌仔 zogˋ geˇ |

## 6. Your Mandarin is a head start: sound correspondences

About half of the core words share their characters with Mandarin, and their readings correspond in regular ways
(measured on the same 1,404 characters; the most common outcome first):

| Mandarin | Sixian | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| h- (before u, o) | **f-** (54%) or h- | 花 faˊ · 火 foˋ · 和 foˇ |
| w- | **v-** (60%), also m-, ng- | 文 vunˇ · 完 vanˇ · 萬 van · 問 mun |
| r- | **ng-** (63%) or no consonant | 人 nginˇ · 日 ngidˋ · 熱 ngied · 肉 ngiugˋ |
| n- before i, some y- and ∅ | **ng-** | 你 ngiˇ · 年 ngienˇ · 牛 ngiuˇ · 二 ngi |
| j-, q-, x- | **g-, k-, h-** (most often) or j-, q-, x- | 九 giuˋ · 期 kiˇ · 戲 hi ·· 千 qienˊ · 心 ximˊ |
| zh-, ch-, sh- | **z-, c-, s-** | 豬 zuˊ · 茶 caˇ · 書 suˊ · 水 suiˋ |
| f- | f-, sometimes **b-, p-** (old readings) | 飛 biˊ · 肥 piˇ · 分 bunˊ |
| d- | d-, often **t-** (Mandarin tone 4) | 大 tai · 地 ti · 弟 taiˊ |
| -n in some words | **-m** | 三 samˊ · 心 ximˊ · 金 gimˊ · 甜 tiamˇ |
| any old "entering" character | **-b, -d, -g** | 十 siib · 八 badˋ · 六 liugˋ · 食 siid · 學 hog |

## 7. One character, two readings

As in Taigi, many characters have an everyday (白讀) and a learned (文讀) reading. Each is introduced where it is
used, and the lessons point out the pair:

| | everyday | learned |
|---|---|---|
| 生 | sangˊ (生日 sangˊ ngidˋ "birthday"; alive, raw) | senˊ (生活 senˊ fad "life", 人生) |
| 行 | hangˇ (行路 hangˇ lu "walk") | henˇ (品行), hongˇ (銀行 ngiunˇ hongˇ "bank") |
| 白 | pag (white) | ped (明白 minˇ ped "understand") |
| 客 | hagˋ (客家, 人客 "guest") | kiedˋ (客觀 "objective") |
| 分 | bunˊ (give: 分𠊎) | funˊ (分鐘 funˊ zungˊ "minute", 分享) |
| 飛 | biˊ (fly; 飛行機 biˊ hangˇ giˊ "plane") | fiˊ (飛機場 fiˊ giˊ congˇ "airport") |
| 無 | moˇ (not have) | vuˇ (無聊 vuˇ liauˇ, also said moˇ liauˇ) |

And one character, two words: **好** hoˋ "good" and **好** hau "to like" (§4 of the grammar inventory).

## 8. Hailu (海陸), written alongside

Hailu is the other big accent of northern Taiwan (新竹, 桃園). Every dialogue line also has a Hailu version; you can
switch accent in the app. Three things change.

**Consonants:** Hailu keeps the zh, ch, sh sounds and has rh- (a voiced, buzzed y): 豬 zhuˋ, 書 shuˋ, 食 shidˋ,
有 rhiuˋ, 一 rhid.

**Tones: seven, and the marks are a code to learn once.** The tone *categories* are the same as Sixian, so every word
maps:

| Category | Sixian | Hailu | Example (Sixian → Hailu) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 陰平 | ˊ 24 | **ˋ 53** (high falling) | 花 faˊ → faˋ · 天 tienˊ → tienˋ |
| 陽平 | ˇ 11 | **(none) 55** | 人 nginˇ → ngin · 來 loiˇ → loi |
| 上聲 | ˋ 31 | **ˊ 24** | 火 foˋ → foˊ · 講 gongˋ → gongˊ |
| 去聲 | (none) 55 | splits: **ˇ 11** (陰去) or **+ 33** (陽去) | 看 kon → konˇ · 大 tai → tai+ |
| 陰入 | ˋ 2 | **(none) 5** | 八 badˋ → bad · 屋 vugˋ → vug |
| 陽入 | (none) 5 | **ˋ 2** | 十 siib → shibˋ · 食 siid → shidˋ |

So most Hailu marks are the Sixian ones swapped; only 去聲 needs the word list (ˇ or +).

**Sandhi** (article 107_015): a 上聲 ˊ syllable becomes + (33) before any tone (點心 diamˊ simˋ → diam+ simˋ), and a
陰入 syllable becomes 陽入 before any tone (菊花 kiug faˋ → kiugˋ faˋ). Strong emphasis can block it.

**Words**: about nine in ten core words are the same in both accents; the rest differ (个 ge / gaiˇ, 這 iaˋ / liaˊ,
恁仔細 / 承蒙 "thank you", 天光日 / 韶早 "tomorrow"). The grammar inventory §17 lists the differences that matter.
